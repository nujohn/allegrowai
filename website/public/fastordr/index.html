For store owners and managers

Your own online ordering, from the customer's phone to your counter.

Customers order ahead, pay in the app and see when their food will be ready, so they shop instead of queueing. Your kitchen gets each order on an iPad, labels and the receipt print by themselves, and your staff cook instead of taking orders. No commission on your own orders.

Why it helps

Food ordered two different ways: with the app, or standing in line

Same bowl, same twelve minutes of cooking. What changes is where your customer spends them — in your line, or still shopping — and whether one of your cooks is stuck at the register.

Standing in line

  1. 12:00She comes to the food court on her lunch break. Six people ahead of her.
  2. 12:03A potential customer sees the line and decides to try a different store.
  3. 12:07She orders, pays, and takes a table to wait, not knowing if she has enough time to buy groceries.
  4. 12:19Her ramen arrives. She needs to eat quickly so she still has time to get groceries before her next task.

Your quickest cook spent that rush at the register, and a table sat full of somebody who wasn't eating yet.

With the app

  1. 11:58She orders from her phone in the produce aisle. Paid. The app says pickup at 12:11.
  2. 11:59It lands on the kitchen iPad. One tap, the label prints, it's cooking.
  3. 12:08She finishes her shopping and goes through checkout.
  4. 12:11She walks over, shows her code, and sits down with her ramen and her groceries.

Same twelve minutes of cooking — she spent them finishing her shopping, fifty feet away. Nobody left the kitchen to take that order.

Same crew, same kitchen, same twelve minutes. The register just isn't in the way any more.

    What your customers can pay with

    Whatever is already on their phone. Stripe shows each customer only the ones they can actually use, so nobody gives up halfway through paying.

    • CardsVisa, Mastercard, American Express, Discover, JCB, Diners Club and UnionPay.
    • Apple PayFace ID and it is done — no card number typed at all.
    • LinkOne tap for anyone who has paid at another Stripe store before.
    • Cash App PayConfirm it in Cash App. Common with younger customers.
    • Amazon PayUses the card already saved in their Amazon account.
    • KlarnaSplits a bigger order into instalments. You are still paid in full.
    • AffirmThe same idea on a large basket — a catering run, a big group order.

    The first five cost you the same 2.9% + 30¢ used in the numbers above. Klarna and Affirm cost Stripe more to run, so they carry a higher rate and are not in those numbers — they are worth keeping for catering, and worth switching off if you would rather not offer them at all.

    Card numbers go straight to Stripe. They never touch your iPad, your printer or your staff, so none of this is yours to keep safe.

    Hardware

    About $520, one time

    You buy the hardware and own it — no rental, no lease. A compatible used iPad with a stand comes to about $150, and both printers are worth having: the Brother labels each individual item, the Star prints the customer receipt. We can supply the whole kit at these prices, tested and paired before it reaches you, so there is nothing for you to shop for.

    Approximate US prices, October 2026: used iPad 9th or 10th generation $110–140 plus a stand or wall mount about $30, so roughly $150 for the screen; Brother QL-810W about $170 (Brother, Staples); Star TSP143IIIBI about $200 used. A new iPad is $349 and a new Star is $330–380 if you would rather not buy used. Supplies are bought as you go. If you already have an iPad, tick the box and the kit drops to about $400. We sell the kit at these prices and do not mark it up.

    Getting started

    Live in an afternoon

    About twenty-five minutes of your time. We do the menu setup, and a person checks it before anything goes live.
